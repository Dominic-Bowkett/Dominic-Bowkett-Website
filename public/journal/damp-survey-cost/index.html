Damp
survey cost
in 2026

An independent damp survey typically costs £120–£800 in 2026, depending on the size of the property and how much of it needs assessing — more again where it's folded into a full Building Survey. The number that matters more than the fee is who's charging it.

Published 19 Aug 2026 Reading time · 9 min By Dominic Bowkett · MRPSA

An independent damp survey — someone attending purely to diagnose what's going on, with a moisture meter and ideally a thermal camera, before any treatment is proposed — typically costs £120–£800 in 2026. A quick check of a single wall sits at the bottom of that range; a full written report on a larger or older property with several affected areas sits toward the top. Have the same diagnosis done as part of a wider RICS Level 3 Building Survey instead, and the figure moves again, to £630–£1,500 or more for the whole survey.

But the price isn't really the number worth dwelling on here. A large share of what gets called a "damp survey" in the UK is a free sales visit from a company that then quotes you the treatment — and that arrangement, not the moisture reading, is usually what decides whether you end up paying for a chemical DPC or a membrane you didn't actually need.

I'm a building surveyor, not a damp-proofing contractor. I diagnose; I don't inject anything or fit a membrane. So an independent damp survey is genuinely part of what I do for a living, not a loss-leader for a quote that follows. What follows is 2026 pricing for the diagnosis itself, why a free survey costs more than it looks like it does, and when it's actually worth paying for a standalone instruction rather than getting the answer as part of a wider survey.

The short answer

Figures below were checked on 19 August 2026 against Checkatrade, MyBuilder and several independent PCA-member surveyors' own published 2026 price guides, cross-referenced against each other. Kent, Sussex, Surrey and London — my patch — trend above the national figures quoted here, in line with the rest of this series.

Those are all paid, independent instructions. The "free" survey most homeowners are offered by a national damp-proofing chain or local specialist doesn't appear in that table because it isn't really priced separately at all — its cost is folded into whatever treatment gets quoted afterwards, which is exactly the arrangement worth understanding before you book one.

What drives the price

Size and how much is affected. You're paying for time on site and how much wall, floor and roof space needs checking, not a flat per-property rate. A small flat with one damp corner is a shorter job than a four-bed house with damp reported in three rooms.

Standalone instruction vs part of a wider survey. A surveyor already on site for a Level 2 or Level 3 survey can assess damp as part of that visit for effectively no extra time; a dedicated damp survey means booking, travelling to and writing up a separate job.

Independent vs "free." A paid, independent survey costs money upfront because that's the only way the surveyor gets paid — there's no treatment margin behind it. A free survey costs nothing upfront because the firm expects to recover it, and then some, from the work it recommends.

The surveyor's qualification. A Property Care Association member holding the CSRT (Certificated Surveyor in Remedial Treatments) or CSSW (Certified Surveyor in Structural Waterproofing) qualification, or a RICS-qualified surveyor, commands a higher fee than an unqualified "damp inspector" — and is the one whose written diagnosis a solicitor, lender or buyer's surveyor is likely to actually rely on.

What the report actually contains. A five-minute moisture-meter reading and a verbal opinion costs less than a proper written report with photographs, a plan of affected areas and thermal-imaging spot checks where they'd help distinguish condensation from a genuine capillary problem.

The free survey that isn't free

The economics are straightforward once you say them out loud. A company offering a no-charge damp survey has to earn a living somehow, and it isn't from the survey. That's not necessarily dishonesty — plenty of PCA members who offer free surveys are perfectly competent at spotting genuine rising or penetrating damp — but the incentive structure only points one way. Nobody at the end of a free visit is paid to conclude "there's nothing wrong here, go home," and the PCA's own code of conduct exists partly because that tension is well understood inside the trade, not just outside it.

The numbers make the case better than the theory does. A paid independent survey at £200–£450 that correctly identifies a blocked air brick or a bridged damp course as the actual cause — rather than a failed damp-proof course — can save you the £1,000–£2,500 a chemical DPC injection typically costs, which I've covered in full in damp proofing cost. And where rising damp genuinely is the diagnosis, getting the method right first time, rather than paying for the wrong one after a rushed free assessment, matters just as much — that's the ground I cover in rising damp treatment cost. Either way, the survey fee is the cheapest part of the whole job, and the one most people try hardest to avoid paying.

Do you actually need a standalone damp survey?

Often, no. If you're already commissioning a RICS Level 2 HomeBuyer Report or a Level 3 Building Survey — covered in full in house survey cost — damp is assessed with a moisture meter as standard practice on both. Paying a second specialist to look at the same wall a surveyor has already checked is money spent twice on one answer.

A standalone instruction earns its fee in a handful of situations. The most common is a mortgage valuation: if the lender's valuer notes suspected damp, some lenders will retain part of the mortgage advance until a specialist damp or timber report is provided, and that report is usually on the buyer to arrange and pay for before completion can proceed on the lender's terms. It's also worth it if you're disputing a contractor's diagnosis before agreeing to treatment, if a previous chemical DPC clearly hasn't worked and nobody's asked why, or if you're selling and want the condition established and on the table before a buyer's survey finds it for you — the same logic behind my Home Insight Survey.

What I wouldn't do is pay for a standalone damp survey and then a full Building Survey separately on the same property within weeks of each other, on the assumption that more opinions equals more certainty. One properly qualified, independent surveyor who has actually walked the whole building is worth more than two partial ones.

What I'd watch for in a quote

  • No free survey from anyone who also sells the treatment. If the same person or firm both diagnoses and quotes the fix, get a second, independent opinion before anything's agreed.
  • PCA membership and CSRT/CSSW, or RICS qualification, checked rather than claimed — ask to see it, the same as I'd want you to check any surveyor's credentials.
  • A written report, with photographs, moisture readings and a plan of the areas checked, not a verbal "it's rising damp."
  • External causes checked first: ground levels, gutters, air bricks and render, before anything inside the wall is proposed as the cause.
  • No pressure to book treatment on the same visit, and a stated turnaround for the written report if you don't get it there and then.

Questions I get asked

How much does a damp survey cost in 2026? A quick check of a single wall typically costs £120–£200. A full independent survey on a 2–3 bed house, with moisture mapping and a written report, runs £200–£450, rising to £450–£800 for a larger, older or more complex property. Folded into a Level 3 Building Survey rather than booked separately, the figure sits within that survey's fee, typically £630–£1,500 or more.

Is a free damp survey worth having? Treat it as a sales visit, not a diagnosis, because that's usually what it is. The firm makes its money on the treatment it recommends afterwards, which is a direct incentive to find something to fix. It can be a reasonable first look at an obviously minor issue, but I wouldn't rely on one alone before agreeing to spend several hundred or several thousand pounds — get an independent, paid-for opinion first.

Do I need a separate damp survey before buying a house, or does my survey cover it? A Level 2 HomeBuyer Report includes a visual damp assessment with a moisture meter as standard, and a Level 3 Building Survey goes further again, so a separate damp survey is usually unnecessary on top of either. It's worth commissioning on its own if your lender's valuation flags damp and specifically requests a specialist report before releasing funds, if you're disputing a contractor's diagnosis, or if you only had a Level 1 report or no survey at all.

How long does a damp survey take? The site visit itself is usually 1–2 hours for an average semi, longer for a larger or more affected property. Most independent surveyors give you the headline finding verbally before leaving, with the full written report, photos and moisture readings following within a few days to a week — worth confirming the turnaround before you book.
