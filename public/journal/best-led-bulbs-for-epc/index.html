Lighting is the cheapest line on an EPC to improve, and I find it unimproved in more houses than I expect. I am usually in a kitchen, with the assessment half done, when I count the ceiling: eight GU10 spotlights, six of them halogen, and a homeowner who is certain they changed all the bulbs. The ones in the fittings you notice they did. The ones in the hallway and the cupboard under the stairs they did not.

The assessment counts fixed lighting outlets — ceiling lights, downlights, wall lights — and how many of them hold a low-energy lamp. Table lamps and anything plugged in do not count. A house with every fixed outlet on LED scores better than one with a mix, and the report will list low-energy lighting for the remaining outlets as a recommendation. It is rarely worth many points, usually one or two, but it costs a few pounds and a ladder, which is more than I can say for most items on the list. The EPC cost guide covers what an assessment involves.

This guide is about choosing the bulb so you do not hate the result. The wrong colour temperature, the wrong cap or a dimmer that does not like LEDs are why people put the halogens back. Smart bulbs are included because people ask, with the honest note that the EPC does not reward them.

How I judge an LED bulb

Is the cap right? Bayonet (B22), screw (E27 or E14), and GU10 spotlight pins are not interchangeable. Take the old bulb out and read the cap before you buy. It is the most common wrong order I hear about.

What colour is the light? Warm white is around 2700K, which matches a filament bulb and is right for living rooms and bedrooms. 4000K is a cool, neutral white that suits a utility room or garage. Pick one temperature per room and keep to it, because a mixed set of bulbs looks like a mistake.

Is it dimmable, and does your dimmer agree? A non-dimmable lamp on a dimmer will flicker or fail early. A dimmable lamp on an old halogen dimmer can still flicker, because old dimmers need a minimum load. If the circuit is dimmed, buy dimmable lamps and expect to change the dimmer too.

How bright is it? Buy by lumens, not watts. A 60W filament bulb is roughly 800 lumens, so look for a pack that states about that. Watts only tell you what it costs to run.

Where is it going? Enclosed fittings, bathroom zones and outdoor lights have rules. Look at the pack for an enclosed-fitting or IP rating before you fit a lamp in a sealed globe, a shower room or an outside light.

Philips Hue White and Colour Ambiance E27

Best smart ecosystem

Smart bulbs are not an EPC measure, but they are an LED that happens to talk to your phone, and people ask. Philips Hue is the established ecosystem, with the widest range of fittings and a long record of firmware updates. The colour bulb is overkill for most rooms, and it is the only one of these I would not buy for an EPC reason.

The EPC cares that the lamp is low-energy, not that it is clever. A smart bulb costs several times a basic LED, and it needs the wall switch left on, or it drops off the network. That is the real-world trap: someone switches it off at the wall and the app stops working.

What actually matters — and what doesn’t

Count the outlets, not the lamps. A five-arm chandelier is one fixed outlet. The EPC wants every fixed fitting to be low-energy; a spare drawer of old bulbs does not count.

Lighting is a small slice of the energy bill. The saving from changing to LED is real, but it is dwarfed by heating. A home energy monitor will show you how small the lighting circuit is, and radiator valves and draught-proofing do more for the money.

Warm white for living spaces. The cold, blue-white lamp that lights an office makes a living room feel clinical. If a homeowner tells me they hated LEDs, the lamp was usually 4000K or 6500K.

Downlights and fire hoods. Replacing a halogen GU10 with an LED in an existing downlight is a like-for-like change. Cutting new holes in a ceiling, or boxing in downlights in a fire-rated ceiling, is a job for an electrician. If a downlight has gone dark, do not assume the bulb: transformers and connectors fail too.

What salesmen push that doesn’t matter. Claimed lifetimes of 25,000 hours or more are the maker’s figures under test conditions. A bulb in a hot, enclosed fitting, or on a poor-quality dimmer, will not reach it. Smart bulbs sold as energy-saving gadgets also save nothing over a plain LED.

Questions I get asked

Will changing to LED bulbs improve my EPC? A little, if some of your fixed lighting is still halogen, CFL or filament. Moving the whole house to LED is typically worth a point or two. It is one of the cheapest recommendations on the report, and it works for the pocket as well as the rating. The EPC cost guide covers the rest of the process.

Do LED bulbs count as low-energy lighting on an EPC? Yes. The assessor counts fixed outlets with LED or other low-energy lamps. Portable lamps do not count, and the figure is a share of fixed outlets, not of bulbs.

What colour temperature should I choose? Warm white (about 2700K) for living rooms and bedrooms, neutral (3000–4000K) for kitchens and bathrooms where you want clearer light, and cooler only for a garage or utility. Whichever you pick, buy a whole room in the same temperature.

Do I need smart bulbs? No. For an EPC a plain LED is enough. Smart bulbs add scheduling and colour, at several times the price, and they need the wall switch left on. They suit a person who wants to control lights from a phone, not someone chasing a better rating.
