Every RdSAP assessment and every building survey I write now ends up as a floor plan in a piece of software, not a hand-drawn sketch pinned to a clipboard. The workflow that actually saves time is walking the house with a phone or tablet running an app — magicplan, CubiCasa, Leica’s own DISTO Plan, Bosch’s MeasureOn — and firing the laser at each wall as you go, with the dimension dropping straight onto the sketch. No transcription, no digits copied off a screen into a spreadsheet an hour later, no reveal measured twice because the first number went missing.

The catch is that a “Bluetooth” badge on the box tells you less than it should. Plenty of units pair over Bluetooth to their own manufacturer app and nothing else — useful if you’re happy living in that ecosystem, a dead end if your survey software expects a universal app like magicplan. The units worth buying are the ones a floor-plan app actually lists as a supported device, not just the ones that happen to have a Bluetooth chip inside.

What follows is a features comparison built from manufacturers’ specifications, the app-makers’ own published device-compatibility lists, and the long tail of user feedback, read with a surveyor’s eye for what a floor-plan job actually needs. It runs from a £70 Bosch that will happily feed MeasureOn to the Leica DISTO D2 that most working assessors reach for.

How I judge a Bluetooth laser measure

Confirmed app pairing, not a Bluetooth logo. The only claim that matters is whether the app you actually use lists the unit as a supported device. magicplan, CubiCasa and the manufacturers’ own apps all publish device lists — check the model against the list before buying, not after.

NFC tap-to-pair, where it exists. On a multi-room job the phone falls asleep, the connection drops, and re-pairing over a Bluetooth settings menu between every room gets old fast. A tap-to-pair NFC chip removes that friction entirely; it’s a small feature that earns its keep on a busy day.

What the app actually receives. Almost every unit here sends a precise dimension into the app for you to sketch a wall from — it does not draw the plan itself. A handful of very new units promise automatic sketch generation, but none of them had a verifiable amazon.co.uk listing at the time of writing; treat that claim with suspicion until it does.

Accuracy and repeatability at domestic range. The same baseline as any laser measure — ±1–2 mm indoors is standard across this list, and a house isn’t built to millimetre tolerances anyway.

Survivability for site use. An IP rating and a drop rating matter more on a loft-hatch job than an extra 50 m of range you’ll never fire indoors.

What actually matters — and what doesn’t

A Bluetooth badge is not the same as a confirmed app pairing. Several genuinely Bluetooth-capable laser measures on Amazon only talk to their own control app — useful for nothing more than reading a number off a phone screen instead of the unit’s own display. Before buying for a specific app, check that app’s own published device-compatibility list rather than trusting the word “Bluetooth” on the listing.

None of these draw the floor plan for you. What crosses over is a precise dimension, dropped into the app so you can sketch the wall it belongs to. A small number of very new units promise automatic plan generation from the laser shots alone — Stabila’s LD 530 BT is one — but none had a verifiable amazon.co.uk listing at the time of writing. Don’t pay a premium for a feature you can’t yet actually buy into on this market.

NFC is a genuine time-saver, not a gimmick. On a job with a dozen rooms, re-pairing over a Bluetooth settings menu every time the phone sleeps adds up. Tap-to-pair removes that step entirely — it’s the single feature in this category most worth paying for if your workflow is genuinely room-by-room.

Green beams earn their keep outdoors, not indoors. The visibility gain over a standard red beam is real in daylight or a bright conservatory. Indoors, at domestic distances, a red beam is perfectly visible and the premium buys you little.

Camera-assisted aiming is for the shot you can’t walk up to. If your work is entirely internal room measurement, it’s money spent on a feature you’ll rarely use. It earns its price on long external runs and awkward sightlines only.

What the listings push that you can skip: raw range past 40–50 m for indoor work, “199 measurement functions”, and internal memory slots — the app is doing the storing now, so a unit’s own memory barely matters.

Questions I get asked

Does a Bluetooth laser measure draw the floor plan for me?

No, not with anything currently sold on amazon.co.uk. What it sends to the app is a precise dimension for the wall you’ve just shot — you still sketch the room shape in the app as you walk it, with each laser reading dropping onto the line you’re drawing instead of being typed in by hand. A few very new units advertise fully automatic plan generation from the laser data alone, but none of them had a verifiable, currently-stocked UK Amazon listing at the time of writing.

Which app should I use — DISTO Plan, MeasureOn, or something universal like magicplan?

Leica’s DISTO Plan and Bosch’s MeasureOn are both free and work well with their own brand’s hardware, so if you’re buying into one ecosystem there’s no reason to look elsewhere. magicplan and CubiCasa are the universal option — both publish a tested-device list covering multiple brands, including several units in this guide — and are worth it if you want the flexibility to mix hardware or move between brands later without re-learning software.

Is NFC pairing actually better than Bluetooth?

For the pairing step itself, yes — tap the phone to the unit and the connection is live, with none of the settings-menu hunting that Bluetooth pairing sometimes needs, especially after the phone has slept or the app has restarted. It doesn’t change the measurement itself, and once paired the two methods behave identically; NFC just removes the friction of getting there, which matters more the more rooms you’re measuring in one visit.

Do I need Bluetooth at all if I’m not drawing a digital floor plan?

No. If your workflow is a tape measure, a laser and a paper sketch, Bluetooth is a battery drain you’ll switch off and a price premium you didn’t need to pay — the plain laser measures covered in the standalone laser-measure guide will do the job for less. Buy into this category only once you’ve actually adopted an app-based floor-plan workflow, or are about to.
