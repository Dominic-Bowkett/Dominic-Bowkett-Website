Repointing a chimney stack typically costs £450–£1,200 in 2026, and £1,000–£1,800 once flaunching, flashing or brick repairs come into it. The mortar is rarely the expensive part — getting safely up to it is.

Published 5 Oct 2026 Reading time · 7 min By Dominic Bowkett · MRPSA

The chimney is the part of the house that gets the worst weather and the least attention. It sits at the highest, most exposed point of the roof, you can barely see it from the ground, and nobody looks at it until a tile slides off, a stain appears on the bedroom ceiling, or a surveyor like me points up with a pair of binoculars. In 2026, repointing a chimney stack typically costs £450–£1,200, with a small, low stack nearer £300 and a big Victorian stack carrying several pots at £900–£1,500 or more.

But that range assumes the repointing is the only job. Chimneys rarely fail in just one place. Once flaunching, lead flashing or a few damaged bricks are added, the bill is more often £1,000–£1,800, and if the brickwork itself has gone, repointing is the wrong answer and a rebuild runs to £1,200–£3,500 or more. Scaffold sits on top of all of it, and on small jobs it can cost more than the repair.

I don't repoint chimneys for a living — I look at them, diagnose what's wrong and say what a sensible repair is, so I have no stake in which of these you choose. This is a straight read on the 2026 numbers and, more usefully, on how to tell which of the three jobs you actually have.

The short answer

Figures below were checked on 5 October 2026 against MyJobQuote, Trade2Base and BestBuilders cost guides, which broadly agree once you allow for what each includes. Where they differ I've shown the spread rather than picking one. Kent, Sussex, Surrey and London sit toward the top of these ranges: the guides put the South East premium at roughly 15–30%.

Flaunching, the sloped mortar cap holding the pots, is priced on its own at roughly £150–£800, depending on the source and the number of pots. Individual replacement bricks are usually £20–£40 each fitted.

What drives the price

Access, above everything. This is the point most price guides bury. On a two-storey house a scaffold for a chimney typically adds £300–£600 on a low, easily reached stack and £800–£2,500 on a tall or awkward one, and the sources I read are blunt that on a small job it can exceed the repair itself. A £400 repointing job sitting on a £1,000 scaffold bill is not unusual. If scaffold is going up anyway for roof, gutter or fascia work, doing the chimney at the same time is the cheapest it will ever be — see my scaffolding cost guide.

Position on the roof. A gable-end stack is generally easier and cheaper to reach than one in the middle of the ridge, where scaffolding has to span the roof or a roof-level platform is needed.

Size and number of pots. A tall stack with four pots is a bigger area, more flaunching and more risk than a single stubby one.

Condition of the brick. Raking out and repointing sound joints is straightforward. Replacing individual bricks adds time and cost, and past a certain point it adds up to a rebuild.

Mortar. Older brickwork wants a lime mortar softer than the bricks around it. Hard cement repointing traps moisture and spalls the brick faces, which is how a £600 repoint turns into a £2,500 rebuild a decade later. It's the same argument I make in my repointing cost guide, only on a stack that has no sheltered face at all.

Repoint, repair or rebuild?

This is where professional judgement earns more than any number above. Repointing only makes sense while the brick faces are still sound. Once frost has spalled the bricks, flaked their faces or cracked them through, new mortar has nothing to bond to, and the job quietly becomes a rebuild whatever the quote calls it.

The signs I look for from the ground, usually with binoculars or a drone: a visible lean; mortar missing in deep, dark joints; bricks with the face blown off; vegetation growing from the stack; white salt staining on the brick (efflorescence); and a stain on the chimney breast or bedroom ceiling below that tracks to the stack. A lean or a horizontal crack is a structural conversation rather than a pointing one — and where a chimney is being taken out entirely, that's a different job with its own risks, covered in the chimney removal cost guide.

The thing I'd pay most attention to is the lead. A damp chimney breast is more often down to failed lead flashing where the stack meets the roof, or cracked flaunching letting rain straight down the flue, than to the mortar joints. A new repoint on a stack with a leaking apron is money spent in the wrong place, and the stain keeps coming. Get the water path diagnosed before agreeing what's repaired. If you can't see the stack safely, a drone survey or roof inspection will show you what's up there for far less than a scaffold.

One more thing worth knowing: an unused chimney that's capped and left unventilated can drive damp into the breast below it. Repointing that stack won't fix that. It needs a vented cap, not mortar.

What I'd watch for in a quote

  • Is scaffold in or out? Quotes differ most here. A low price that excludes scaffold is not a low price, and you want it itemised so you can compare like with like.
  • Mortar specified. On pre-1920s brickwork, ask for lime or a lime-rich mix, not a hard sand-and-cement default.
  • Flaunching and flashing assessed. A repointing quote that never mentions either on an old stack hasn't properly looked at it. Ask for photographs of the problem areas before and after.
  • No pressure from a doorstep roofer. A cold caller who has noticed your chimney needs urgent work, and offers a discount to start today, is a familiar pattern. Get written quotes from roofers who can show NFRC, TrustMark or CompetentRoofer registration.
  • Brick replacement priced per brick, with a limit, so a £700 repoint can't quietly become £1,900 on the day.

Questions I get asked

How much does it cost to repoint a chimney? In 2026 repointing a chimney stack typically costs £450–£1,200, with a small low stack nearer £300 and a large Victorian stack with several pots £900–£1,500 or more. If flaunching, lead flashing or damaged bricks need attention at the same time, £1,000–£1,800 is more realistic. Scaffold is the big variable and is often priced separately.

Do I need scaffolding to repoint a chimney? On a two-storey house, almost always. Safe access to a stack usually means a scaffold tower or a chimney scaffold, which adds roughly £300–£600 on a low or easily reached stack and £800–£2,500 on a tall or awkward one. Rope access and cherry pickers exist but are rarer, and a roofer working off a ladder is not a quote I would accept.

When does repointing become a rebuild? When the brick faces have failed rather than just the mortar. Once frost has spalled the bricks, the stack leans, or it is cracked through, new mortar will not hold it, and you are looking at a partial or full rebuild at £1,200–£3,500 or more. Repointing a stack that really needs rebuilding is money spent twice.

Is chimney repointing the same as flaunching? No. Repointing renews the mortar joints in the brickwork; flaunching is the sloped mortar cap on top that holds the pots and sheds water. They are usually done together, but flaunching is priced separately, at roughly £150–£800 depending on the source and the number of pots.
