Pigeons are something I see often from the top of a ladder or on a drone feed and rarely in a client’s brief. A heap of droppings on a parapet, a nest in the lee of a chimney, a gutter that has turned into a bird table: none of it is dramatic until water has nowhere to go, a downpipe is blocked solid or a buyer sees the staining on the elevation and starts to wonder what else has been left.

Solar panels have made the problem more common. The gap between a panel and the roof is warm, dry, sheltered and out of sight, which is exactly what a pigeon wants. Once birds are in there, the droppings and nesting material pile up under the array, can foul cable runs, block the drainage path and make any later panel work a miserable job. That is why proofing kits exist, and why this guide covers both the mesh that closes the gap and the spikes that stop birds perching in the first place.

Two honest points before the products. Proofing is a barrier, not a cure: birds that cannot nest here will try next door. And the law on wild birds is real. Most species, and any nest in active use, are protected under the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981, so do not disturb a nest with eggs or chicks; fit proofing outside the nesting season or when the nest is empty. If the array is already occupied, a specialist pest contractor is the sensible call. For what an array costs in the first place, see solar panels cost.

How I judge bird proofing

Does it close the way in? Birds go where they can get in. On a solar array that is the gap under the panels, so a mesh skirt does the real work. Spikes only stop birds standing on something. If the problem is a nest under the array, a spike strip on the front edge is not the answer.

How is it fixed? Clips that grip the panel frame, or adhesive and screws on a ledge. I would avoid anything that means drilling the roof covering or the panel frame, because that is where leaks and warranty queries start. Check the panel maker’s or installer’s guidance if you are unsure.

What is it made of? Stainless outlasts galvanised, and both outlast plastic. On a high, exposed roof you will not want to be back in two seasons, because the cost of a second visit usually exceeds the saving on the first kit.

Does it suit the surface? A strip designed for a flat coping stone is no good on a curved sill. Match spike width to ledge depth, and check that adhesive will bond to the material, as old limewash, dusty stone and frosted brick all defeat it.

Can you fit it safely? This matters most. Most of this kit is fitted at roof edge or gutter height. If it cannot be done from a safe platform or tower, it is a job for a competent roofer or solar installer, not a weekend on a ladder.

What actually matters — and what doesn’t

Mesh for solar, spikes for ledges. That one rule covers most jobs. A pigeon nesting under an array is a gap problem. A pigeon roosting on a parapet is a perch problem. Buying the wrong one is the commonest waste of money in this category.

Clean first. Droppings carry fungal spores and are unpleasant to handle. Wear a respirator mask and work gloves when clearing old nesting material, and bag it for disposal. Fit the proofing only after the space is clean, or you are sealing the mess in.

Look before you climb. You can often see the extent of the problem from the ground with a pair of binoculars, and a drone survey will show the back slope and the chimney without anyone going up. Work out where the birds are getting in before you buy a roll of mesh.

Do not forget the gutters. Nesting material and droppings block outlets, and a blocked gutter is a damp problem waiting to happen. Gutter guards and a gutter vacuum help, and the guttering replacement cost guide is the comparison if the damage is done.

What salesmen push that doesn’t matter. Ultrasonic repellers, plastic owls, hanging CDs and gels that birds learn to ignore within weeks. Anything sold as a one-visit, guaranteed result with no mention of the gap under the panels deserves a second question.

Questions I get asked

What is the best way to stop pigeons nesting under solar panels? Close the gap with a clip-on wire mesh kit fixed along the lower edge of the array. It does not need drilling, and stainless steel is the longest-lasting option. Spikes alone will not stop birds getting underneath.

Will bird proofing affect my solar panel warranty? Clip-on kits are designed to avoid drilling or touching the panel itself, and are sold on that basis. I would still check the installer’s or panel maker’s guidance before fitting anything, and keep any invoice from the person who fits it.

Do bird spikes hurt birds? Spike strips are blunt deterrents designed to stop birds landing, not to injure them. They only work if the spacing suits the ledge, because birds that can land between the points will nest among them, which is a worse outcome than no spikes at all.

Can I remove a pigeon nest myself? Not while it is in use. Most wild birds and their active nests are protected under the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981. Wait until the nest is empty, then clear it with proper protection, or call a pest controller. Then proof the space.
