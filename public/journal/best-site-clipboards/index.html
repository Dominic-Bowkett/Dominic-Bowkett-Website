A surveyor writes standing up. A roof edge, a loft hatch, a garden path, the top rung of a stepladder in a kitchen. Notes made on a knee in a conservatory are the notes you can’t read at the desk, and a notebook alone gives you one wobbly surface. A clipboard gives the paper a rigid back, a place for the pen and a way to hold a form still in a draught.

The search term is huge, and almost all of it is school and office stationery. Site use is narrower: the clipboard has to cope with weather, drops off a ladder and being slung in a van with a pair of boots. Storage clipboards with a lid, waterproof cases and the choice between A4 and foolscap are the questions that matter here. For the paper itself, the waterproof notebook guide is the companion; for the digital route, the rugged tablet guide covers when the clipboard goes away entirely.

Very little in this category is expensive, so the real cost of a wrong choice is a soaked set of notes. Everything below is judged on that: does it keep the paper dry and flat long enough to get the job written up.

How I judge a site clipboard

Weather protection. A flap cover shrugs off drizzle and fails in a downpour. A sealed case or lidded storage board does better, at the price of weight. Think about how wet your average day gets before paying for it.

Size, and the A4 versus foolscap question. Foolscap is longer than A4 and still turns up in older forms. Buy for the paper you use. A board that is 10mm too short leaves the bottom of every sheet flapping, and an American letter-size board leaves a margin of unsupported paper at the foot of an A4 sheet.

Storage. A hollow board holds a spare pen, a tape and a pad. That is useful in a loft where you can’t keep going back to the bag. It also adds weight and bulk, so it suits people who work out of one hand.

Clip strength. A weak clip lets a stack of five sheets slide out in a breeze. A sprung metal clip, or a rubberised one, grips better than moulded plastic. Press the clip open and shut a dozen times before trusting it.

Fit in the bag. A storage clipboard is deeper and will not slide into every tool backpack pocket. Check the pocket before buying a thick board.

What actually matters — and what doesn’t

The surface and the clip are the clipboard. A flat, stiff board and a clip that holds are most of it. Everything else is a convenience. If the board flexes, your handwriting suffers; if the clip is loose, your notes end up in next door’s hedge.

Pick for the forms you use. If you print A4, buy A4. If your firm still uses foolscap, buy foolscap. Do not pay extra for a feature you will not use.

Skip the gimmicks. Built-in calculators, ruler edges moulded into plastic, and clipboards marketed with a tablet holder add cost and are rarely better than a separate item. A battery-powered light on a clipboard is a nice idea until the battery is flat in the van.

When a clipboard is the wrong answer. If your surveys already run on a tablet, a clipboard is a spare for the days the screen can’t be read in sunlight or the battery is flat. For loft-only EPC work with a tablet in hand, a pocket notebook may be all you need.

Questions I get asked

Is a storage clipboard worth it? If you carry forms, a pen and a small pad, and often work somewhere you cannot easily put a bag down, yes. The lid keeps the sheets together and flat, which cheap folders do not. If you only carry a couple of sheets, the lighter folder is plenty.

Are waterproof clipboards actually waterproof? Treat the claim carefully. Sealed cases and weatherproof boards are designed for wet fieldwork, but listing wording varies from “weatherproof” to “water resistant” to “waterproof” with no standard behind it. Read recent owner feedback and look for what actually keeps the paper dry, not just the title.

A4 or foolscap for site notes? A4 for anything printed today, foolscap only if your forms are in that format. A4 is more common, so replacement boards and sleeves are easy to find. If you scan or photograph your notes afterwards, A4 is simpler.

Can a clipboard replace a tablet? For some jobs, yes, and paper has the advantage of not dying at 10% battery. For others, a tablet saves the re-typing. Many surveyors keep a clipboard as the reserve, which is the cheapest backup in the van.
