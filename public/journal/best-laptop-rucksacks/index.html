A survey day puts a lot of electronics in one bag: a rugged tablet or laptop, a charger, a power bank, a laser measure, a thermal camera and a tangle of cables. Most of it gets carried from the van to the front door, across a client’s hall and up to a loft hatch. A flimsy bag with no padding turns a dropped rucksack into a dead screen.

The market is mostly sold to commuters. That suits report days, viewings and desk work, but a survey bag also has to cope with a wet driveway, a muddy boot and being set down on a dusty landing. The picks below run from a cheap sleeve-and-pocket bag to a structured premium pack.

How I judge a laptop rucksack

Padding where it matters. A laptop sleeve raised off the bottom of the bag protects the screen when the rucksack is dropped on a hard floor. A flat sleeve touching the base does not.

Fabric and zips. Zips fail first. Look for chunky zips and a fabric that wipes clean, because it will be put down on dirty floors.

Carry comfort. A tablet, charger and power bank is heavier than it sounds. Wide, padded straps and a back panel stop the load digging in by the end of the day.

Weather. Water-resistant fabric copes with a short shower. For a long walk to a property in the rain, a rain cover or a genuinely waterproof bag is the safer option.

Security. A hidden pocket or lockable zips are worth having if the bag will sit in a parked van or on a station platform, but they are an extra, not a reason to choose a bag.

What actually matters — and what doesn’t

Matters: a padded, raised laptop compartment, strong zips, and shoulder straps that cope with a full day’s load. Check the stated laptop size against the actual machine, because the size is the screen diagonal and not the width of the case.

Doesn’t: USB charging ports, which still need a power bank inside the bag, and the litre capacity on its own. A bigger bag only means a heavier bag.

If you carry heavy hand tools, a laptop rucksack is the wrong bag for them: tools wear the fabric and press into the laptop sleeve. Keep them in a tool bag or van box and keep the rucksack for the electronics.

Questions I get asked

What size laptop rucksack do I need? Check the stated maximum laptop size against your own machine, then allow for a charger and a tablet. A 15.6" rucksack is the common fit, but a rugged tablet and its accessories take more room than the laptop.

Is a laptop rucksack waterproof? Most are water-resistant, which means they cope with a shower, not a soaking. If your day involves long outside walks, add a rain cover.

Can I use a laptop rucksack as a tool bag? It is not ideal. Tools abrade the fabric and put sharp loads against the laptop sleeve. Use a dedicated tool bag for hand tools.

Are anti-theft rucksacks worth it? A hidden pocket and lockable zips make casual theft harder, but they will not stop someone who takes the whole bag. Do not leave it visible in a parked van.
