Draught-proofing is the cheapest thing on an EPC recommendation report, and usually the one homeowners actually go and do. A worn door seal, an unused chimney still pulling air up it, a loft hatch with a gap all the way round — none of it costs much or needs a tradesman. It needs buying the right product for the actual gap, not one tub of mastic and good intentions.

One thing worth saying first: draught-proofing and ventilation are not the same job. A trickle vent, air brick or extractor fan is doing a job on purpose — sealing it up along with the gaps you actually want gone is how well-intentioned draught-proofing makes a house worse, not better. Everything below targets gaps that are there by accident: doors, hatches, an unused chimney, a letterbox and keyhole never meant to be airtight.

Here’s what I’d buy, job by job — not ranked against each other, since the right product depends on which gap it’s going into.

How I’d prioritise a draught-proofing kit

Accidental gaps first, deliberate ventilation never. A gap around a door, hatch or unused chimney is there by accident. A trickle vent, air brick or extractor fan is doing a job on purpose — sealing it up too is the most common way draught-proofing goes wrong.

Match the seal to the gap. Brush pile tape suits 3–5mm; a bottom door seal handles up to 25mm; a loft hatch needs a seal rated for that frame. The wrong product for the gap size is why most draught-proofing jobs don’t work.

An unused chimney is usually the best single job here. A flue that isn’t swept, lined or lit is one of the largest uncontrolled openings in an ordinary house, and sealing it is often the biggest-feeling improvement here for the lowest cost — provided it’s genuinely not in use.

Small, cheap fixes add up. A letterbox brush and keyhole cover together cost less than one glazing film kit, and both sit at head height in rooms people stand in. Don’t skip them for one bigger-ticket item.

Don’t expect an EPC-band jump from any of it. Draught-proofing is nearly always the cheapest recommendation on an EPC report, cost-effective almost by definition — but the SAP points it earns are modest. Worth doing regardless; it rarely moves the letter alone.

What actually matters — and what doesn’t

Percentage claims on the packaging are for that one gap, not the whole house. A chimney balloon tested to block a stated majority of airflow, or a radiator reflector rated to a lab percentage, measures one product in isolation. A house with six draughty gaps and one fixed still has five.

A whole-house kit rarely fits a whole house. Boxed multi-packs assume a standard door and window size an older house often doesn’t have. Buying per gap, measuring first, beats one kit that’s the wrong length for half the doors it’s meant for.

Sash windows need a different answer to a stick-on strip. Self-adhesive brush pile on a sash’s beading rarely lasts; proper sash draught-proofing fits brushes into machined grooves in the frame, a specialist joinery job, not a DIY kit item.

Fitting quality matters more than the spec. A brush seal stuck to a dusty or damp frame lifts within weeks regardless of how good it is. Clean and dry the surface first — the real cause of most draught-proofing that “didn’t work”.

Questions I get asked

Will draught-proofing my house cause condensation? Only if it seals something ventilating the house on purpose — a trickle vent, air brick or extractor route — rather than an accidental gap. Damp or misting afterwards means the house relied on that gap for ventilation more than anyone realised.

How much will this improve my EPC rating? Draught-proofing is one of the cheapest items on a typical EPC recommendation report and almost always cost-effective — but the SAP points earned per measure are small, and it rarely moves the letter band alone.

Do I need to draught-proof a chimney I never use? If genuinely disused — not swept, not lined, nothing lit — yes, and it’s usually the best single job here for the money. Never fit a balloon or wool pad to a flue still in use, and remove it before lighting one.

Will brush pile tape fix a draughty sash window? Not properly. Stuck to the beading it lifts within a season or two; proper sash draught-proofing fits brushes into machined grooves in the frame, a specialist joinery job, not a stick-on strip.
