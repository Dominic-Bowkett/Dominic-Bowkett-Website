A fire door that does not close is just a door. In flats and houses in multiple occupation I find the same three faults again and again: a closer that has been removed because it slammed, a wedge or a hook holding the door open, and strips that have been painted over until they no longer touch the frame. Each one defeats the point of the door, which is to hold smoke and fire back long enough for people to get out.

The law is plain on the principle. The Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 puts a duty on the responsible person to keep escape routes safe, and in England the Fire Safety (England) Regulations 2022 added specific duties for residential buildings with two or more sets of domestic premises: check flat entrance doors annually, and communal fire doors quarterly in taller buildings. A self-closing device is normally part of what makes a door a fire door. The fire risk assessment cost guide covers who does the assessment and what it picks up.

Before you buy anything, one warning. Closers and strips only help on a door that is genuinely fire-rated, typically a 30-minute FD30 or FD30S leaf, hung in a matching frame with correct gaps. Fitting a closer to a hollow bedroom door does not turn it into a fire door. How I judge a fire door closer or strip

Is it stated to a standard? A door closer for a fire door should be CE or UKCA marked to BS EN 1154 and suitable for fire doors. A seal should state a fire rating and the door rating it is tested for. If a listing only talks about “heavy duty” it tells you nothing.

Does it match the door? Closers have power sizes and are rated by door width and weight. Strips come in set widths for set grooves. Measure the door and the gap before you click.

Does it close the door every time? A closer has to overcome the latch, so it needs a latch-speed setting. Too weak and the door stops ajar; too strong and tenants wedge it open.

Is the fitting compatible with the certificate? Concealed closers and strips fitted into grooves involve cutting the door. Check the door’s certification, or ask the fitter, before you cut.

Can you see whether it works? An assessor, or a landlord doing a quarterly check, wants to see the door close from fully open without a push. A visible closer helps.

A concealed closer is mortised into the top edge of the door and the frame, so nothing shows when the door is shut. Lorient is a UK maker of door seals, and its strips combine an intumescent element, which swells in heat to close the gap between door and frame, with a blade or brush that holds back cold smoke. What actually matters — and what doesn’t

The door matters more than the hardware. A solid, certificated FD30S door in a well-fitted frame with a closer and strips is a fire door. A cheap closer on a hollow door is not. If you are not sure what you have, an assessor or a fire door inspector can tell you from the door’s edge, its gaps and any certification plug or label.

Gaps decide whether strips work. Typical guidance puts the gap between door and frame at around 2–4mm; a bigger gap lets smoke through whatever strip you fit. A door that has dropped on its hinges needs rehanging before it needs new seals.

Cold smoke seals matter. Intumescent strips alone seal only once heat swells them. A smoke blade or brush deals with the early cold smoke, which is what reaches people first. Look for a strip described as fire and smoke.

Check the whole set. Hinges, latch, glazing and the closer all belong to the same assembly. Mixing in unrated parts weakens it. If a flat entrance door has been replaced with a modern composite door, that is worth looking at. For the rest of the compliance kit, see smoke and heat alarms, fire blankets and extinguishers and the first-time landlord kit.

What salesmen push that doesn’t matter. Finish and colour, “heavy duty” wording with no standard behind it, and hold-open devices fitted without a link to the fire alarm. A hold-open that is not tied to the alarm is just a wedge with a nicer name.

Questions I get asked

Does a fire door have to have a closer? Generally yes. A fire door is meant to be closed in a fire, and in a rented flat or HMO that means a self-closing device. A fire risk assessment will state which doors need one.

Can I fit intumescent strips to any door? You can stick them on, but they only help a door that is a fire door in the first place. Fitting them to an ordinary internal door does not give it a fire rating.

Are overhead or concealed closers better? Overhead closers are easier to fit, check and replace. Concealed ones look better but need cutting into the door, which can affect its certificate. For most lets I would choose overhead.

How often should fire doors be checked? In England, residential buildings with two or more sets of domestic premises must have flat entrance doors checked at least yearly, and communal doors quarterly in buildings over 11 metres. A landlord of a smaller HMO should still look at them regularly; see the fire safety guidance for your property type.

Fire doors are one of the things I look at in a rental or a conversion, and a missing closer or painted-over strip is a quick fix before it becomes a licence or insurance problem.
