roof sealants

Most of the roof leaks I am asked about arrive with a tube of sealant already used on them. Someone has found the stain, gone up a ladder or leaned out of a window, and put a black bead along whatever looked wrong. Sometimes it works for a season. More often it hides the entry point, sends water sideways under the covering, and gives a roofer a dirty surface to work around.

So this guide is not a list of things that will mend your roof, because none of them will. It is a list of the products that do a short, specific job honestly: tape across a failed joint, a bead at a gutter or fascia, a flexible edge on lead flashing, a brushed coat on tired flat-roof felt. Tape bridges and liquids coat. Know which one you are buying, and know that a slipped slate, a cracked tile or a rotten deck needs a roofer, not a cartridge.

Before you spend anything, look from the ground first with a pair of binoculars, and keep off the roof unless you have the right access, such as the roof ladders covered elsewhere. If the roof is old enough that you are weighing repair against replacement, the new roof cost guide sets out what the alternative really costs.

How I judge a roof sealant

What it is being asked to bridge. A hairline crack, a moving joint and a missing tile are three different problems. A tape or fibre-reinforced coating can span a crack; a bead of sealant mostly cannot, and none of them can replace a tile.

Surface and temperature. Every datasheet says the same thing: clean, dry, sound substrate, and application above about 5°C. A leak found in a rainstorm is rarely repaired in the right conditions, which is why so many stopgaps fail by spring.

Compatibility with what is underneath. Silicone on lead is fine; bitumen on some single-ply membranes is not. Read the substrates list. If the product is not named for your roof covering, assume it is not suitable.

Flexibility. Roofs move with temperature. A rigid filler cracks at the first cold snap. Look for published low-temperature flexibility figures rather than adjectives.

How hard it is to undo. A roofer will have to remove your repair before doing the real one. Black mastic on slate or clay tile, or on a listed building, is a particular problem. Keep the area small.

The picks in detail

01

Evo-Stik Flashband

Best overall stopgap

Flashband is a self-adhesive bitumen strip with a foil face that bonds by hand pressure to most roofing materials, with a stated life of at least 15 years and a service range of −10°C to +100°C. It is the nearest thing in this guide to a proper patch rather than a smear, because the tape bridges a gap instead of relying on a sealant staying stuck to a moving joint. Dry, clean, primed surfaces matter more than the product. On a wet tile in November it will not hold.

For

  • Bridges a crack or joint rather than filling it
  • No tools or cartridge gun
  • Wide material compatibility on the manufacturer’s datasheet

Against

  • Needs a dry, clean surface and ideally primer
  • Visible, and difficult to remove neatly later

02

Everbuild Black Jack Flashing Tape

Best narrow-width alternative

Black Jack is the same idea from a different maker: aluminium foil over a bitumen rubber adhesive, pressed into place by hand. The published data gives a tape strength of 3.5N/mm and flexibility at −15°C without cracking, with an application range of 5°C to 40°C. That last figure is the one people ignore. Applied in a frosty January, it will look fine and fail in the spring. The 75mm roll suits a ridge or a short abutment better than a wide flat-roof seam.

For

  • Published strength and low-temperature flexibility figures
  • Handy width for ridges and short abutments
  • Foil face resists sunlight better than bare bitumen

Against

  • Cannot be applied below 5°C
  • Not a repair for a slipped or cracked tile

Evo-Stik Roof and Gutter Sealant

Best budget pick

A highly flexible, rubberised black sealant sold for gaps around flashings, between roof panels and tiles, and around fascia boards. The maker quotes waterproofing for up to 10 years. For a garage roof overlap or a gutter joint that weeps, it is a few pounds and an evening. For a leak you cannot explain, it is a few pounds spent on the wrong end of the problem. If water gets in at one point and shows at another, caulking the stain’s nearest edge fixes nothing.

Best for lead flashing edges

I include it because lead flashing is where a good half of the roof stains I am shown originate, and because it is the correct kind of product for that edge: it stays flexible and does not attack the lead. It is still a sealant. If the lead has lifted, split or been dressed badly, the proper repair is lead work.

What actually matters — and what doesn’t

Find the entry point first. Water travels. A stain on a bedroom ceiling can sit a metre or more from where it came in, following a rafter, a purlin or a felt lap. Sealing the spot above the stain is the commonest mistake I see. Check the flashing at the chimney, the valley, the abutment with the neighbour’s wall, and the ridge and verge, in that order.

A sealant is a stopgap. A bead of anything on a roof is a repair with a shelf life. If the cause is a slipped tile, a split flashing or a perished felt, the sealant delays the work and adds cost to it. Photograph what you did and tell the roofer.

Flat roofs and old felt. Coatings can extend the life of a flat roof that is sound underneath. They cannot rescue a roof with a wet, saturated deck. If the ceiling below is damp in more than one place, or the roof has ponding that never clears, think about replacement rather than another coat. A building survey will say which it is.

Gutters. Sealing a leaking gutter joint is a reasonable few-pound job. If the whole run is failing, the guttering replacement cost guide is the honest comparison, and a gutter vacuum is the cheaper way to find out whether it is only blocked.

What salesmen push that doesn’t matter. “Guaranteed for 10 years”, “permanent repair” and “one coat”. Warranties assume ideal substrates and application, and a roof in use is rarely that. Spend instead on a primer and a dry day.

Safety. The ladder is the dangerous part, not the product. Do not work from a ladder onto a slate or tile roof without proper stabilisation, never walk on a roof you have not checked, and if the repair is above a single storey, pay someone with the equipment.

Questions I get asked

Can I fix a leaking roof with sealant? For a small, well-located gap at a gutter, a fascia or a lead edge, a sealant can stop a drip for a while. For a leak you cannot trace, or one caused by a tile, felt or timber fault, it will not, and it can make the real repair harder.

Is flashing tape or sealant better for a roof leak? For a crack or joint, tape, because it bridges the gap and does not rely on a thin bead staying bonded. For sealing lead into brickwork or a small overlap, a sealant. Both need a dry, clean surface.

How long does roof sealant last? The published figures run from around 10 years for cartridge sealants to 15 for tapes, but those assume a sound substrate. On a roof that is moving, getting wet or lying in shade, I would plan on a few seasons and put a proper repair in the diary.

Can I use roof sealant in winter? Many products need application above roughly 5°C and a dry surface. Black Jack, for example, states an application range of 5°C to 40°C. In winter a temporary cover and a roofer is better than a bead that never cures properly.
