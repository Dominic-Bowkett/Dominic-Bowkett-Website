Roof tile
replacement
cost in
2026

Replacing a few slipped or broken roof tiles typically costs £170–£450 in 2026, and re-tiling a whole pitch £1,500–£4,000. The tiles cost pennies; the access and the diagnosis are what you pay for.

Published 6 Oct 2026 Reading time · 7 min By Dominic Bowkett · MRPSA

A tile has slipped, or cracked, or there is a gap on the slope where one used to be, and you want to know what it costs to put right. In 2026, replacing a few roof tiles typically costs £170–£450 as a call-out, or £80–£200 for a single tile with easy access. The tile itself is £1–£8. Everything else on the bill is the roofer getting to it and turning up at all.

That is the cheap end. Once the problem is a patch rather than a tile, the figure moves to £500–£1,400 for around 10m², £1,500–£4,000 for a whole pitch, and £5,500–£12,000 for re-tiling an average semi. The art is knowing which of those you actually have, because a repair that treats a failing roof as three loose tiles is money spent twice.

I don't lay tiles for a living — I inspect roofs, diagnose what's wrong and say what a sensible repair is, so I have no stake in which you choose. What follows is the 2026 pricing and, more usefully, how to tell whether patching is sensible or false economy.

The short answer

Figures below were checked on 6 October 2026 against BestBuilders, Trade2base and MyJobQuote cost guides, all updated between June and August 2026. They broadly agree; where they differ I've shown the spread. Kent, Sussex, Surrey and London sit toward the top: the guides put the South East premium at roughly 12–35%, depending on the source and the job.

Ridge tile work sits alongside these: £200–£550 for repointing or repairing, and £300–£1,000 for 10m of ridge, depending on whether it is repointed or replaced with a dry-fix system. Lead flashing repairs are £200–£700.

Per tile or per m²?

Two pricing logics get mixed up on this topic. For a handful of tiles, you pay a call-out: the tiles are £1–£8 each, but nobody comes out for £8, so the minimum charge sets the price. For anything bigger, the price is by area. A full re-roof runs roughly £100–£170/m² in concrete and £140–£220/m² in clay, and tiles alone are about £10–£25/m² for concrete and £30–£70/m² for clay. Membrane and battens add £8–£16/m².

The consequence is that the unit price falls sharply the more you do at once, because scaffold and set-up are largely fixed. That is why a roofer who quotes £300 to replace six tiles, and then £2,800 to do the whole slope, may be giving you a better deal on the second than the first.

What drives the price

Access, above everything. A single tile from a ladder on a single-storey extension is the bottom of the range. The same job on a two-storey house, with the tile on the wrong side of the ridge, needs scaffold. Scaffold typically adds £450–£1,500 and is a fixed cost, rising to £900–£2,500 on a two-storey house when it is for a full re-roof. If scaffold is going up for other work anyway, tile repairs at the same time are at their cheapest — see my scaffolding cost guide.

Tile type and matching. Modern concrete tiles are cheap and easy to match. Clay costs more, and discontinued, handmade or reclaimed tiles can be hard to find and cost far more per tile. Plain tiles need roughly 20–40% more tiles per square metre than interlocking ones, which raises the labour too.

What's under the tiles. Replacing a tile is quick. Discovering that the battens beneath are rotten, or the underlay has perished, turns a tile job into a strip-and-refix.

Pitch and complexity. Steep roofs, dormers, valleys and hips are slower and riskier, and the guides add to the price for them.

Region. London and the South East run higher: one guide puts a 35m² partial re-tile at £2,800–£3,700 in the South East against £2,500–£3,300 in the South West baseline.

Patch or re-tile: when repairing is false economy

This is where a surveyor's eye earns more than any number above. A roof is a system. Individual tiles shed the water but the battens hold them, the nails or clips fix them, and the underlay is the second line of defence. A tile can slip because a storm lifted it, in which case replacing it is the right fix. Or it can slip because the batten it hangs on has rotted, the fixings have corroded, or the tile is delaminating. Then a replacement slips too.

Signs I look for that say "this isn't a loose tile": a whole area of slipping, not a single tile; tiles spalling or flaking, with a sandy or layered surface; a visible sag or dip in the line of the roof; daylight in the loft; stained or rotten battens; and moss thick enough to hold moisture against the tile all winter. Most of these show from the ground with binoculars, or from the loft with a torch. The age of the roof matters too: as one of the guides puts it about felt roofs, a patch on an ageing covering is buying time, not solving the problem. If you are spending a quarter of a re-tile on repairs every couple of years, you should be pricing the re-tile.

The opposite error matters too. A single storm-slipped tile on an otherwise sound roof does not justify a £6,000 quote for a new roof, and a roofer who proposes one on the strength of a missing tile is selling, not diagnosing. If you can't tell which you have, an independent look costs far less than guessing — a roof inspection or a drone survey will show you the whole slope. And where the roof leaks around a stack, the tiles are often not the cause; the lead is, as I cover in the chimney repointing cost guide.

What I'd watch for in a quote

  • Scaffold in or out? A low price that excludes it is not a low price. Ask for it itemised.
  • What's being replaced. Tiles only, or battens and underlay too? Ask for photographs of the batten condition before and after.
  • Matching tiles. On an older roof, ask where the replacement tiles come from and whether they will match, rather than accepting whatever is in the van.
  • Dry-fix ridge and verge. If the ridge is being redone, a dry-fix upgrade is around £400–£900 over mortar and generally lasts longer; mortar-only repointing buys 5–10 years.
  • No pressure from a doorstep roofer. A cold caller who has spotted "a few loose tiles" and can start today is a familiar pattern. Get written quotes from roofers with NFRC, TrustMark or CompetentRoofer registration.
  • A price for the extras. Replacement battens, extra tiles and flashing should be priced per unit up front, so a £400 repair can't quietly become £1,400 on the day.

Questions I get asked

How much does it cost to replace a roof tile? In 2026 replacing one or two broken or slipped tiles typically costs £80–£200, and a call-out for a handful of tiles £170–£450. The tiles themselves are £1–£8 each; you are paying for the roofer's minimum charge and for access, which is the biggest variable.

How much does it cost to re-tile part of a roof? Roughly £500–£1,400 for a patch of about 10m², and £1,500–£4,000 for a whole pitch of 25–45m², including underlay, battens and scaffold. A full re-tile of an average semi is £5,500–£12,000.

Is it worth repairing a roof with slipped tiles? If the roof is sound and the tiles have simply let go in a storm, yes. If they are slipping because the battens are rotten, the tiles are delaminating or the fixings have corroded, patching is false economy and you are likely to be back within a year or two.

Do I need scaffolding to replace roof tiles? For a couple of tiles on a low roof, a roofer with a roof ladder can often manage without. On a two-storey house, scaffold is usually needed and adds £450–£1,500, and it is a fixed cost, which is why the per-tile price falls sharply the more you do at once.
