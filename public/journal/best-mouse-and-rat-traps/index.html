Half the roof voids I enter have had a visitor. You can tell without seeing anything: a trail of small black droppings along the top of a party wall, a greasy smear where a body rubs the same timber each night, a nest of shredded insulation in the corner of the eaves. In a Victorian terrace the signs are in the suspended floor void instead, and a borescope through a missing airbrick often shows more than the owner wanted to know.

A surveyor does not catch rodents for a living, but we are the ones who find the evidence and the way in. Mice chew cable sheathing, and rats will go through a lead or a plastic pipe, so it is more than a nuisance. Gnawed wiring is a fire risk, and a rat in a drain is usually a defective drain, which is a job for a drainage survey. The survey defect cost guide covers what putting right the underlying defect typically involves.

This guide is about the traps and the proofing. A trap is a short-term answer: it removes what is in the building. The long-term answer is to find the hole and close it. Both belong in the same shopping basket, and I have picked products with that in mind.

How I judge a rodent trap

Is it sized for the animal? A mouse trap will not kill a rat, and a rat trap is far too heavy for a mouse. A wrongly sized trap injures the animal, and a wary rat will not return to the spot. Identify what you have first: mouse droppings are about the size of a grain of rice, and rat droppings are nearer a small olive stone.

Is it humane and legal to use? Spring traps for rodents should be a design approved for the purpose, and live-catch traps need checking often. Glue boards are cruel and I would not recommend one. If an animal is caught alive and in distress, release or dispatch it quickly.

Can pets and children reach it? Set traps in a covered run, a box or behind a barrier. A loose trap on an open floor is a risk to a toddler and a dog, and one in a loft can be dislodged and drop through a ceiling.

Can you set and clear it safely? This matters more than people expect. If setting is awkward, you will not set enough traps. Plastic designs with a lever or a bait cup get used more often than wooden ones.

Does it deal with the cause? A trap with no proofing is a treadmill. Anything that lets you seal the way in is worth more than another trap.

The picks in detail

01

Little Nipper Mouse Trap

Best overall

For mice, the plain snap trap is still the answer, and the Little Nipper is the design most people mean by the term. It is a small wooden-base trap with a sensitive pedal, it kills fast, and it is cheap enough to set a dozen in one go. Setting several along the runs matters far more than which brand you pick, and this one is easy to buy in quantity.

Pest-control suppliers have sold this pattern for decades, and owner feedback is consistent that it is reliable when baited sparingly with something sticky such as chocolate spread or peanut butter. A pea-sized dab works better than a lump, because a mouse that can lick the bait without touching the pedal walks away with a free meal.

  • Fast and straightforward when set correctly
  • Reusable if you tip the catch out

Against

  • You have to deal with the dead mouse
  • Fingers and a bit of practice needed to set it

Big Cheese Rat Trap

Best snap trap for rats

Rats need a bigger, stronger trap, and a mouse trap will only injure one and teach it to avoid the spot. The Big Cheese rat trap is a large spring trap sold specifically for the job and widely stocked. Treat it with respect: it is built to kill a rat, which is also a good reason to keep it away from children and pets.

Rats are wary of anything new, so the usual advice from pest controllers is to leave the trap unset and baited for a couple of days first, then set it. Put it in a covered run along a wall, and in a loft use a board so it does not sit loose on the insulation.

Rentokil Rat Zapper

Best premium for a clean kill

An electronic trap lures the animal into a tunnel and delivers a high-voltage shock, with an indicator light to say it has caught something. The attraction is that you do not handle anything messy: you tip the body out and reset it. For a landlord clearing a void between tenants, or a homeowner who cannot face a snap trap, it earns its price.

05

Humane Live Catch Mouse Trap

Best for catch and release

Humane traps catch the mouse alive so you can release it. They suit people who will not use a kill trap, and they are widely sold in tip-over and tunnel designs. Models vary a lot, so read the owner feedback on the specific one you pick and check it has a good door catch.

The catch is the practicalities. A trapped mouse needs checking at least every few hours, because it can die of stress or hunger, and a mouse released near the house will walk straight back in. Take it a few hundred metres away, and still seal the hole, or you are running a revolving door.

For

  • No kill
  • Reusable
  • Good for a single mouse in a kitchen

Against

Rentokil Rat Bait Station

Best for a garden or outbuilding

A bait station is a lockable plastic box that holds rodenticide so pets and children cannot reach it. It is the tool pest controllers use for a rat problem outside, on a run along a garage wall or beside a shed. The box matters as much as the bait: bait left loose is a hazard to dogs, cats and wildlife.

I would use it outdoors only, and not in a loft or under a floor. A rat that eats anticoagulant bait takes several days to die and often does so in a cavity, where it smells for weeks and brings flies. If you are not confident, call a pest controller, and read the pack, because current rules restrict how rodenticides may be used and where.

Xcluder Rodent Control Fill Fabric

Best for sealing the way in

Traps deal with what is in the house, but proofing deals with why it is there. Xcluder is a stainless-steel and poly fill that you push into gaps around pipes, cables and airbricks, so rodents cannot chew through it the way they do foam or mortar. It is the product I would put in the basket alongside the traps.

Mice can get through a gap of about 6mm, roughly the width of a pencil, and rats through around 15–20mm, so the hole you ignore around a waste pipe is a front door. Fill the gap, then finish it with mortar or a suitable sealant. A rodent that cannot get in is a better answer than one you catch.

What actually matters — and what doesn’t

Find the way in. Mice need a gap of about 6mm and rats about 15–20mm. Look at where pipes and cables pass through the wall, at missing or damaged airbricks, at gaps under eaves and at the line where a lean-to meets the house. A head torch and a borescope show you more than a trap ever will.

Number beats brand. Mice explore short distances, so set traps every metre or two along the wall, with the trigger end against the skirting. One expensive trap in the middle of a floor catches very little.

Check often and dispose of the catch properly. Wear gloves, bag the animal, and wash your hands. Rodents carry disease, and droppings and urine are the reason to wear a mask when you clear a loft.

Rats outside usually mean a drain. If you see rats in the garden and not in the house, look at the drains first. A broken or disused pipe is a motorway for them. That is a job for a CCTV drainage survey, and your local council may be able to help.

What salesmen push that doesn’t matter. Ultrasonic repellers are the main offender. Pest-control bodies are consistent that they do little to move established rodents, and I would not spend money on one. Peppermint oil and similar home remedies fall in the same category.

Poison is a last resort. It kills slowly, the rodent dies where you cannot reach it, and pets and wildlife can be poisoned in turn. If the problem is serious, call a professional rather than scattering bait.

Questions I get asked

What is the best trap for mice in the house? A pack of snap traps set along the walls where you see droppings, baited with a pea-sized dab of chocolate spread or peanut butter. Set several, check them daily and keep going for a week after the last catch.

How do I get rid of rats in the loft? Trap them with a rat-sized trap in a covered run, and find how they got up there, usually a gap at the eaves, a pipe or a gnawed hole in a soffit. The loft is the symptom; the route in is the cause. If you cannot find it, a surveyor or pest controller can.

Do ultrasonic repellers work? Not in any way I would rely on. Animals tend to get used to the noise, and it does not carry through walls or insulation. Sealing the entry points and trapping what is already inside is what works.

Is a humane trap as good as a snap trap? It is as good at catching a single mouse, but it needs checking often and the animal can return if released too close. If you have an infestation rather than a stray, a snap trap is quicker and, on balance, kinder than a mouse that slowly starves in a box.
