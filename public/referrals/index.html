Who it’s for

Your client asks “what should we do to the house?” This is the answer.

Building surveyors

Your survey tells the buyer what’s wrong with the house. I take it from there: I work from your defects report (and, with your permission, your photos), so the client isn’t paying twice, and turn it into a plan for energy upgrades done in the right order.

Architects

Clients planning an extension or renovation often want the house warmer at the same time. A retrofit survey before the design is fixed shows what the existing fabric needs, where the moisture and ventilation risks are, and how the upgrades fit your scheme.

Domestic energy assessors

Your EPC client asks what they should do next, but retrofit isn’t part of your accreditation. Refer them to me: I’ll use your EPC as the starting point and pay you 20% of my fee.

Estate agents

Buyers ask about the EPC, the heating bills and what it would cost to improve the house. Refer them to an independent assessor who will tell them honestly what the house needs. As with any referral fee, tell your client you receive one.

Letting agents and landlords

Landlords need to know which upgrades will lift their EPC, ahead of the planned move to a minimum of C for rented homes, and what they will cost. I give them a plan for the property that improves the rating without causing damp, so the work is done once. You earn 20% on each one, and on every property in a portfolio.

What the survey covers

Measured on the day, not guessed from a checklist.

Energy performance

A full energy assessment of the house as it is, including whether the current EPC is right and how it compares with the current methodology.

Ventilation

Extract fans and cooker hoods measured in litres per second against Approved Document F; trickle vents and door undercuts checked before the house is made more airtight.

Moisture and condensation

Humidity and surface readings room by room, so insulation never traps damp. Where the client already has a defects survey, I work from it.

Thermal imaging

If required, and when conditions are suitable (a cold day with the heating on): missing insulation, draughts, cold bridges and weak spots, shown in pictures the client can understand.

Roof and fabric

Loft and floor voids and wall construction checked by borescope. A drone survey of the roof if needed, when conditions are suitable.

A plan for the house

Occupancy and future plans, then upgrades in a sensible order over the short, medium and long term, with indicative costs.

I follow PAS 2035 principles throughout. This is a private survey for the homeowner rather than a lodged assessment; if a funding scheme later needs a lodged PAS 2035 assessment, I can do that too.

Tools and qualifications

I carry out every survey myself, with the same calibrated kit each time.

Testo 417 vane anemometer
Extract fan and cooker hood flow rates in l/s, tested against Approved Document F.
FLIR thermal imaging camera
Heat loss, draughts and cold bridging, if required and when conditions are suitable.
Protimeter MMS3
Relative humidity, dew point and non-invasive moisture readings.
Borescope
Inspection of cavities and floor voids through small holes in mortar joints.

Room and opening dimensions for the energy model and floor plan.
